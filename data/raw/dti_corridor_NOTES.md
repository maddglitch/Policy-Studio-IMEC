# Digital Trade Integration (DTI) — corridor notes

**Source:** DTI database, European University Institute (Ferracane et al.), as reported in
Ferracane, Lee-Makiyama & van der Marel / "Global Trends in Digital Trade Policies and
Practices," *World Trade Review* (2026), 25, 127–151. 163 economies, 65 indicators, 12 pillars.
Figures here are policy counts (2023). The live DTI map (dti.eui.eu) is blocked on the UAE
network; figures taken from the open-access published paper. Per-economy index (DTRI) is the
next release; bulk dataset on EUI Cadmus (handle 1814/76462).

**Why this matters:** this is the regulatory root cause of the corridor's weak "cross-border"
step. The single window is committed (WTO TFA 100%) and largely built (UNTFS), but cross-border
paperless trade requires cross-border DATA to move — and that is exactly what the corridor's
anchors restrict most.

**Key findings (cross-border data flows, Pillar 6):**
- Only 8 of ~163 economies impose >6 restrictive cross-border-data policies. THREE are corridor
  anchors: **Indonesia (11), Saudi Arabia (9), India (8)** — alongside China (18), Korea, Turkey,
  Algeria, New Zealand.
- **Binding FTA commitments to OPEN cross-border data flows: South Asia = NONE, MENA = NONE.**
  i.e. India and the entire Gulf have made zero binding commitments to let trade data cross
  borders. (EAP has ~10; EU/North America yes.)

**Regional restriction averages (global avg 34.5 policies/country):**
- South Asia (India): 55.7 — most restrictive region; India alone = 82.
- MENA (Gulf, Jordan): 44.6 — 2nd most restrictive; Oman lowest in region (30).
- East Asia & Pacific (ASEAN): 41.4; Indonesia 74 (2nd most restrictive economy globally).
- EU: 33.8; highest on *enabling* policies (17.1 vs global 11.1).

**The CEPA tie-in (the thesis hook):** the DTI says no MENA or South Asian economy has a binding
open-data commitment — yet the UAE's CEPAs (India, Indonesia, Israel, Vietnam) contain
cross-border data-flow provisions. The UAE is thus creating the *first* binding cross-border
data commitments in a region that otherwise has none — the regulatory crack through which the
corridor's cross-border layer can be built, outward from the UAE.

**Coverage caveat:** the UAE and Qatar are not in the paper's country-level MENA sample; the MENA
regional figure stands in for the Gulf neighbours. Worth confirming UAE's own DTI score from the
full dataset/Cadmus before it's load-bearing.
